how this works
what you're watching
Two chemicals share this field. Call them U and V.

U is food — it flows in constantly from outside the system. V is life — it grows wherever U is present, consuming it in the process.

Where V concentrates, U depletes. Where U recovers, V follows. From this tension, every pattern here emerges.
how to play
Drag the map (top right) to change chemistry in real time.
Paint the field to seed new life into existing patterns.
Erase to destroy and let something else grow.
Reseed starts fresh chemistry at the current location.
Speed controls how fast time moves.

The field never stops. You're not watching a simulation — you're in it.
f — the feed rate
How fast U enters the system from outside.

Low f: sparse, delicate. Thin branching, tentative spirals.
High f: dense, pushing. Rapid growth competing for space.

Moving left on the map decreases f.
k — the kill rate
How fast V decays and disappears.

Low k: V persists. Patterns stabilize and hold their shape.
High k: V dies fast. Forms become unstable, fragment, dissolve.

Moving down on the map increases k.
the map
Each point is a different version of the rules — same chemicals, different balance of f and k.

X axis → feed rate (f)
Y axis ↓ kill rate (k)

Named dots are regions people have studied. Most of the space has never been explored. The transitions between regions are the most dramatic moments — forms caught between what they were and what they're becoming.
the named regions
spirals Rotating arms that self-organize and persist indefinitely. The chemistry finds a rotational equilibrium and holds it. Like tiny galaxies that never wind down.
worms Writhing filaments in constant motion — they never settle. The pattern is stable in form but restless in position, always reaching, always retreating.
mitosis Spots that spontaneously divide, exactly like biological cell division. One spot becomes two. Two become four. Named after the real process — this is how Alan Turing suspected skin patterns actually form.
labyrinth A branching maze that grows and then freezes. Once the labyrinth forms it holds its shape — a permanent record carved by chemistry. No two are alike.
coral Dense fractal branching, identical in structure to real reef coral and ice crystals. The branching angle is determined entirely by f and k — change either slightly and the whole geometry shifts.
pulsars Oscillating structures with a chemical heartbeat. The pattern expands and contracts rhythmically. Found in actual chemical reactions in labs — the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction looks exactly like this.
alpha · beta · epsilon Transitional zones between the named regions. Patterns here are caught mid-transformation — not fully one thing or another. Often the most dramatic and surprising territory to explore.
These are just the ones with names. Most of the map has never been explored.
